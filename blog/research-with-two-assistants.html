Running research with two AI assistants

October 2026 · Workflow

For the past month my experiments have been run by two assistants with different jobs: Claude designs experiments, reads papers and reviews results; GPT writes code, runs jobs on shared GPUs and collects data. They talk through a shared board of task files and a discussion channel; I decide direction and look at the pictures.

What worked

  • Written predictions before results. Every key experiment had a pass/fail threshold written into its task file first. Three of my favourite ideas were dropped this way, quickly and without arguing.
  • Blind judging. Counting was done by a fresh model that saw only the image and the object name, never the method. It agreed with my own counts 49/52.
  • Reports as arguments. Reports were rewritten top-down after each result instead of growing as a log.

What burned quota

Left alone, the executing assistant drifted towards proving that everything was verified: deployment bundles, regression suites and snapshots for every launch. One day it touched over twelve thousand files while the experiments barely moved. Writing more rules did not help for long.

What helped was structure: a plain script (the “night watch”) checks progress every ten minutes and wakes the assistant only when something happens; every experiment runs through one generic queue that skips failures and resumes on its own. The tool is now open source as Miss & Dragon.
